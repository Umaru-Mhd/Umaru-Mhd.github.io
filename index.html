About Me

I am a doctoral researcher in the Future Ecologies Group Lab at the School of Design, Southern University of Science and Technology. My research connects biophilic and adaptive design, indoor air quality, indoor environmental quality, building performance simulation, and intelligent sensing for sustainable built environments.

My current PhD work investigates how nature-based design strategies and adaptive building systems can be sensed, simulated, and optimized to improve indoor environmental health while reducing energy demand and operational carbon.

Before and alongside my doctoral pathway, I lectured in the Department of Building Engineering at Imam Abdulrahman Bin Faisal University, contributing to lecture delivery and research in sustainable building practices.

Research Interests

My research is positioned between human-oriented environmental design, performance-based simulation, and ecological intelligence in buildings.

Biophilic and Nature-Based Design

Restorative indoor environments, ecological design strategies, vegetation integration, and human-centered spatial quality.

Adaptive Buildings

Climate-responsive envelopes, adaptive systems, integrated facades, passive strategies, and low-carbon performance pathways.

IAQ and IEQ

Indoor air quality, ventilation effectiveness, indoor environmental quality, thermal comfort, and occupant-centered evaluation.

Simulation and Sensing

CFD airflow analysis, daylight studies, building performance simulation, IoT sensing, and data-informed design workflows.
